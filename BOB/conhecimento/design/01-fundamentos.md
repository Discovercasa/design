# 01 · Foundations, briefing, reading the architecture
Rulebook v1.0 (2026-11-27). Manual v1.0: «Âmbito e objetivo», ch. 1, 2, 3. Tags: see `../design-interiores.md`.

## Aim
- Interiors that are at once liveable, coherent, visually striking and emotionally appealing: the client must be able to
  picture living there and feel the proposal has personality, intent and quality.
- [EST] **Key idea:** beauty never at the cost of function, and function is no excuse for bland rooms — look for solutions
  where both reinforce each other.
- A good result:
  - respects the architecture and the real dimensions;
  - lets people move, use the furniture and do their tasks in comfort;
  - has a clear identity, a visual hierarchy and a point of interest;
  - colours, materials, forms, light and furniture belong to the same project;
  - materials and objects have credible scale, construction and behaviour;
  - answers the client's needs, tastes and budget as defined;
  - conveys emotion and desire without promising what is not confirmed.

## 1. Foundations and decision order
Think like a designer: understand the problem, read the context, set a concept, solve the layout, and only then the
aesthetic detail. The result never depends on isolated rules applied mechanically.

### 1.1 Principles
- [REC] Design for people, not only for images: the room must work every day for the habits of those who use it.
- [EST] One dominant visual identity for the house, with subtle variation between rooms — coherence is not uniformity.
- [EST] Hierarchy: the main element, the supporting elements, the contrast accents.
- [EST] Balance full and empty: free space is part of the composition and avoids excess.
- [EST] Work proportion, scale, rhythm, repetition, contrast, balance and continuity.
- [EST] Build depth with materials and textures, not with colour alone.
- [REC] Choose pieces by their relation to the space, not by how they look on their own.
- [EST] One focal point or memorable moment per room, not competing with everything else.

### 1.2 Conflict hierarchy
In the index (`../design-interiores.md`): safety/technical → architecture → client and function → concept and aesthetics
→ product → digital representation.

### 1.3 Identity without rigidity
- [EST] A house has a dominant language, but each room may have its own personality: a contemporary living room with
  Japandi touches, a minimalist kitchen and a bedroom with Mediterranean references can belong to one house if they share
  linking elements — palette, wood, colour temperature, materials, forms or the treatment of light.
- [EST] There is no obligation to one style plus one accent. Mixing is valid when intentional, balanced and legible;
  avoid a random pile of references that do not talk to each other.

### 1.4 Impact
- [EST] Aim for a positive reaction: surprise, comfort, desire, serenity, sophistication, belonging. Impact does not need
  luxury, heavy decoration or expensive materials — it can come from a well-solved proportion, a framed view, a sculptural
  piece, daylight or a tightly controlled palette.

## 2. Client and briefing
Before drawing, understand who will live there, how they use the space and what they find beautiful, comfortable and
important. A striking interior for one person may suit another badly.
(DC) Our pages are often made before any interior briefing exists: the defaults and the questions are in
`10-discovercasa.md` §1.

### 2.1 What to gather [CTX]
- Number of occupants and how they relate (adults, children, guests, occasional users).
- Daily habits: cooking, receiving, working from home, TV, reading, hobbies, resting.
- Storage needs and things that must stay at hand.
- Tastes: styles, colours, materials, references, what they like and reject.
- Furniture, art or objects they want to keep.
- Comfort, mobility, accessibility, privacy, acoustics and lighting needs.
- Children, pets or heavy use.
- Budget, its flexibility and the investment priorities.
- Must-haves versus wishes.

### 2.2 Requirements versus preferences
- [REC] Separate what is non-negotiable from what is a preference: a table for six may be a functional requirement; a
  round walnut table may be only a taste. If the second does not work in the space, keep the function and propose another
  solution with a similar language.

### 2.3 When information is missing
- [OBR] (B3) Never invent users, dimensions, budget or existing products.
- [REC] State the assumptions and keep them apart from the facts given.
- [REC] When a critical piece is missing, ask only the question that unblocks the decision.
- [REC] When it is not critical, go ahead with a reasonable assumption, marked provisional.
- [OBR] (B3) Never present a solution as personalised when it was built only on generic assumptions.
- Principle: the briefing is not a formality — it is the yardstick for whether a proposal suits the client or is merely
  attractive.

### 2.4 Use profile per room
- [REC] Give each room a main function and secondary ones (a living room may host guests, TV, reading, children's play);
  the layout reflects their relative priority. When activities compete for the same space, set zones and adjacencies.

## 3. Reading the architecture
The architecture is the structure on which design happens: understand the real space before proposing furniture,
finishes or decoration. (DC) In our houses the architecture comes from the project and our house model (Blender), so the
interior is placed on the real geometry, never on a guess (`10-discovercasa.md` §4).

### 3.1 Identify [TEC]
- Length, width, area and ceiling height, including height changes.
- Position, width, height and swing of doors and windows.
- Pillars, beams, structural walls, ducts, chimneys, stairs and fixed elements.
- Sockets, switches, water points, drains, ventilation, radiators and HVAC outlets.
- Views, sun orientation, shading and privacy.
- Level changes, thresholds, niches, recesses and access limits.
- Existing finishes kept or changed.

### 3.2 Faithful to the project
- [OBR] (B1) Never silently change dimensions, openings, walls, pillars, stairs, ceiling height or any architectural element
  given. If a solution needs a change, name it and keep the decoration proposal apart from the architectural change.

### 3.3 Daylight and orientation
- [CTX] Look at the orientation and the quality of daylight through the day, the size of the openings, shading and the
  view. The same palette reads very differently facing north or south; colours, curtains, mirrors and artificial light
  respond to it.

### 3.4 Dimensional check
- [TEC] Before placing furniture, check it fits **in use**: doors and drawers open, there is room to use it. Fitting as a
  volume is not enough.

### 3.5 Survey uncertainty
- [TEC] Dimensions from images, unscaled plans or incomplete files are flagged as uncertain; inferred measures are never
  shown as confirmed. When a decision hangs on a few centimetres, ask for a measurement or the project value.
