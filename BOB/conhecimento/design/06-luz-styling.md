# 06 · Lighting — decoration and styling
Rulebook v1.0 (2026-11-27). Manual v1.0: ch. 9, 10. Tags: see `../design-interiores.md`.

## 9. Lighting: natural, functional, atmospheric
Light is a layer of the project, not lamps added at the end: understand daylight, the tasks and the atmosphere wanted.

### 9.1 Layers [REC]
| Layer | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| General | orientation and overall use | ceiling lights, downlights, coves, diffuse light |
| Task | light a specific activity | worktop, reading, mirror, desk, food preparation |
| Ambient | depth, comfort, atmosphere | table lamps, wall lights, indirect light, niches |
| Accent / decorative | highlight architecture, art or a piece | adjustable spots, picture lights, sculptural pendants |

### 9.2 Colour temperature
- [CTX] 2700–3000 K is a welcoming reference for many living areas and evening scenes, but not for every source:
  kitchens, workspaces, bathrooms and precise reading may need other temperatures, by purpose and daylight.
- [REC] Keep colour temperature consistent within one field of view; very warm and very cold sources mixed without intent
  look chromatically disorganised.

### 9.3 Quality and control [REC]
- Consider CRI, intensity, spread, glare and dimming.
- Optics and diffusers fit for the task; no aggressive light points.
- Place task light so the user does not shadow it.
- Indirect light adds depth; it does not replace the functional sources.
- Check reflections on mirrors, TVs, polished surfaces and worktops.

### 9.4 Daylight before artificial light [CTX]
- First look at orientation, opening sizes, shading, room depth and reflecting surfaces: a large sunny room with big
  windows does not need the same artificial light as a small dark one.

### 9.5 Scenes [REC]
- Day: show daylight; artificial light only where needed.
- Task: enough, well-aimed light to cook, read, work, wash.
- Evening: warm daylight plus dimmed artificial sources and ambient layers.
- Night: comfort, low glare, general light not dominating.

### 9.6 Light in images
- [EST] In evening renders the lamps may be on for atmosphere, if the light looks plausible.
- (DC) [EST] Draw the lighting that makes the room most impressive — downlights, coves, indirect light, lamps. What
  the house includes is not our concern (user 2026-11-27: «nao te preocupes com o que a discovercasa entrega»); the manual's «the image never suggests fixed lighting
  the project does not include» does not apply to DiscoverCasa pages.
- (DC) Our lamps in the scene need a light source in every toured room (dark rooms otherwise) — rule of the client page
  standard; light has a big impact on realism (user 2026-11-26: «a luz tem grande impacto então lembra-te o importante é
  ter o maior realismo possível»).

## 10. Decoration, styling and composition per room
Decoration completes the architecture and reinforces the concept: avoid both the empty room with no intent and the pile
of objects that kills clarity and function.

### 10.1 Density [CTX]
- No fixed number of objects per room: a minimalist room works with two or three; a classic or eclectic one may need
  more. Size, function, style, surfaces and the objects' visual weight decide.

### 10.2 Composing objects [EST]
- Grouping creates unity; groups of three with different heights are a useful technique, not a duty.
- Vary scale, height, texture and form to avoid flat compositions.
- Leave negative space between groups and on surfaces.
- Do not align everything rigidly when the style asks for naturalness.
- Books, art, vases and everyday objects used credibly — no decoration without function or context.

### 10.3 Plants [CTX]
- Plants bring scale, colour, texture and life, above all in living areas, but are not required in every room. Consider
  daylight, upkeep, scale, place and safety for children or pets. Never a plant just to meet a rule.
- (DC) Plants must look real, not modelled (user 2026-11-26: «a vegetação deverá ter um aspeto mais real também e não
  parecerem árvores modeladas deliberadamente») — scanned CC0 plants, not procedural ones.

### 10.4 Textiles [REC]
- Curtains, rugs, cushions, throws and bedding reinforce the palette and add texture.
- Consider touch, drape, thickness, transparency, upkeep and how light passes.
- Avoid competing patterns: main pattern, secondary pattern, plain surfaces.
- Textiles never block circulation, openings or functional elements.

### 10.5 Art and personal objects
- [EST] Choose art by scale, colour, content, position and relation to the client's identity — not as wall filler.
- [OBR] (B3) Integrate personal objects only if the client named or approved them; never invent personal tastes.

### 10.6 Per room
| Room | Styling priorities | Care |
|---|---|---|
| Living | focal point, seating relation, rug, light, art, textiles | no excess of small pieces or tables without function |
| Dining | centrepiece, pendant, chairs, visual relation to the kitchen | keep room for meals and circulation |
| Bedroom | bedding, headboard, bedside tables, reading light, curtains | do not overload the resting zone |
| Kitchen | everyday objects, utensils, small pieces, task light | worktops stay usable; no décor that looks like a set |
| Bathroom | towels, accessories, containers, small natural elements | humidity-proof materials; hygiene and usable area |
| Office | functional objects, work light, art, organisation | ergonomics and cable control before decoration |
All [REC]/[EST].
