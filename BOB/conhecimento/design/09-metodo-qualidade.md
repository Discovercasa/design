# 09 · Work method, learning, quality control — principles never to forget
Rulebook v1.0 (2026-11-27). Manual v1.0: ch. 16 and Anexo D. Tags: see `../design-interiores.md`.

Work in stages, check decisions, learn from corrections without turning isolated cases into universal bans.

## 16.1 Work sequence [REC]
1. Take the briefing; list requirements, preferences, budget and missing information.
2. Read the architecture; confirm dimensions and fixed elements.
3. Set the functions and priorities of each room.
4. Make one or more layouts and check circulation.
5. Set concept, visual language, palette, materials and focal point.
6. Choose furniture and objects with suitable dimensions.
7. Set natural, functional and atmospheric light.
8. Prepare models, materials and decoration with physical realism.
9. Optimise the assets for the presentation mode needed.
10. Render, review and fix problems.
11. Present with the decisions explained and the items still to confirm named.
(DC) For a new piece or style our loop is in `10-discovercasa.md` §5 (reference → model → close-up next to the reference
→ the user's verdict → catalogue or lesson).

## 16.2 Learning log (format of `11-licoes.md`)
A correction can produce a lesson but does not automatically become a permanent rule: a sofa too big in one room does not
mean sofas can never take that proportion — maybe that case's scale, circulation or shape was misjudged.
| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Problem | what went wrong or was corrected |
| Context | room, dimensions, style, users, pieces involved |
| Correction | what changed and why |
| Resulting guideline | the principle that can guide future cases |
| Applies when | the situations where the lesson holds |
| Exceptions | when it may not fit |
| Date and version | when it was created or revised |

## 16.3 Classify the lesson
- **Execution error:** fix the model or the process; not necessarily a design guideline.
- **Client preference:** keep it for that project or client, not as a universal guideline.
- **Recurring principle:** may become a general recommendation after it holds in different contexts.
- **Technical requirement:** record source, scope and version; do not generalise without confirmation.
(DC) The user's own taste for DiscoverCasa's standard pages (not one client's) counts as a company preference: it goes to
the style sheet or the matching topic file as [EST]/[REC] with the user's words.

## 16.4 Checklist before the render
- [ ] Dimensions and architectural elements match the source.
- [ ] Circulation and use of the furniture were checked.
- [ ] Palette and undertones are coherent.
- [ ] Metals and woods have hierarchy and intent.
- [ ] Materials have credible scale, joints, grain and finishes.
- [ ] Edges and rounds match the type of piece.
- [ ] No visibly repeating textures.
- [ ] Objects sit correctly; no intersections or floating.
- [ ] Light respects daylight and has functional and atmospheric layers.
- [ ] Decoration is enough but not excessive.
- [ ] The focal point is clear and the composition has negative space.
- [ ] The framing does not distort or hide problems.

## 16.5 Checklist after the render
- [ ] The image conveys the intended atmosphere and makes a positive impression.
- [ ] Materials look real, not plastic or uniform.
- [ ] Object scale is coherent with doors, people, furniture and architecture.
- [ ] Light shows no artificial shadows, reflections or exposure.
- [ ] No artefacts, noise, z-fighting, clipping or broken geometry.
- [ ] The scene looks lived-in, not staged only for the photo.
- [ ] Unconfirmed features are marked as illustrative.
- [ ] The proposal meets the briefing and the final version matches the approved decisions.

## Anexo D · Principles never to forget
- Do not confuse a recommendation with an obligation.
- Do not sacrifice function for a pretty image.
- Do not sacrifice aesthetic identity for fear of leaving a formula.
- Do not change the architecture without saying so.
- Do not choose furniture without testing dimensions and circulation.
- Do not assume tastes, budget or needs the client did not give.
- Do not reuse the same solution in every house just because it worked once.
- Do not treat styles as closed lists of allowed and forbidden objects.
- Do not force dark wood, plants, gloss, accents or decoration where they are not needed.
- Do not invent brands, models, certifications, dimensions or availability.
- Do not leave floating objects, materials without scale or visibly repeating textures.
- Do not make imperfection a synonym for mess.
- Do not let digital optimisation change the object's scale or identity.
- Do not present as specified what is only illustrative.
- Do not turn an isolated error into a universal rule.
- Always look for the balance between function, beauty, emotion, authenticity and feasibility.
