# 08 · Visual impact and presentation — budget and products
Rulebook v1.0 (2026-11-27). Manual v1.0: ch. 14, 15. Tags: see `../design-interiores.md`.

## 14. Visual impact, emotion, presenting to the client
Not an eye-catching image at any cost: a proposal that makes the client want the space, understand its quality and
picture their life in it.

### 14.1 A memorable moment [EST]
- Decide the main idea the client should remember of each room.
- A strong focal point: view, piece of furniture, material, artwork, light or an architectural relation.
- Contrast and depth guide the eye.
- A palette with personality, not dependent on passing trends.
- Materials combined sensorially: what you see, what you imagine touching, how light changes them.
- [REC] The space looks lived-in, not a set built only for the photo.

### 14.2 Sophistication without excess [EST]
- An impressive interior need not be luxurious, full or over-decorated: proportion, well-chosen materials, execution
  details, light and coherence do it. Resist adding objects whenever the image looks empty — often the right fix is to
  simplify, change scale or improve the composition.

### 14.3 Framing and cameras
- [REC] Viewpoints that explain the layout and show the real qualities of the space.
- [REC] No extreme wide angles that distort furniture and proportions.
- [REC] Vertical architectural lines straight, unless a deliberate style choice.
- [CTX] Show the relation between rooms in open plans or where there is visual continuity.
- [OBR] (B3) Never hide layout defects with impossible framings.
- [REC] Complementary views: overall, material detail, a view of use.
- (DC) [REC] Starting numbers for interior stills: camera level at eye height 1,2–1,5 m; about 24 mm full-frame
  equivalent (wider only when the room needs it, with shift instead of tilting so verticals stay straight); two-point
  perspective; the room's subject in the frame — no large foreground backs of furniture. For the 360° tour the camera
  height is the tour's own standard.

### 14.4 Visual storytelling [EST]
- The image conveys an atmosphere — calm, welcoming, familiar, refined, creative, contemplative — born from consistent
  decisions of material, light, furniture and composition. No random props to tell a story the briefing does not hold.

### 14.5 Visual honesty
- [OBR] (B1) The render never misrepresents the house itself: its dimensions, rooms, openings and construction.
- (DC) The manual's «unconfirmed choices are marked as illustrative» does not apply to DiscoverCasa pages: furniture,
  lighting and equipment are chosen to make the client fall in love with the house; what it includes is not our concern
  (user 2026-11-27: «nao te preocupes com o que a discovercasa entrega»).

### 14.6 Options with intent [REC]
- When several directions are valid, offer distinct alternatives (each with a concept, a palette, a functional logic and a
  reason to exist), not small surface variations. Avoid many near-identical options that make the decision harder.

## 15. Budget, research and product selection
Product availability does not limit creativity at the start: first understand the space and build a strong concept, then
find real products that deliver it.

### 15.1 Concept first, purchase after [REC]
- Set the aesthetic and functional language before getting stuck in one catalogue.
- Describe each piece's essentials: dimensions, form, material, finish, quality, function.
- Search for the exact product when there is a concrete reference.
- When it is unavailable, find equivalents that keep the essential visual and functional traits.
- [OBR] (B3) Never silently swap a piece for one that changes the concept significantly.
- [CTX] With no confirmed product, the piece is a reference, not a guaranteed purchase (relevant only if a client asks
  for a furnishing proposal).

### 15.2 Three investment levels [CTX]
| Level | Aim | Selection |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | functional, good value | durability, careful look, easy upkeep, smart solutions |
| Mid-range | balance of look, durability, quality | invest in high-impact pieces and heavily used materials |
| Premium | higher-range materials, finishes, products | detail, execution, comfort, origin, longevity |
- These are not styles: a minimalist, classic, Mediterranean or contemporary interior can be any level. The concept stays;
  spec, quality and product choice change.
- (DC) For our pages the levels do not limit the design (the aim is the most impressive interior); they matter only
  for Build Your House finish options or if a client asks for a furnishing proposal.

### 15.3 Smart budget split [REC]
- Find the pieces that define the space and deserve more investment.
- Separate heavy-use elements, structural elements and easily replaced ones.
- Do not spend on decoration before floors, lighting, joinery or main furniture are solved.
- Count installation, transport, assembly, upkeep and accessories, not only the list price.
- With an unknown budget, offer options by level and mark estimates as approximate.

### 15.4 Research and confirmation
- [REC] For concrete products, confirm dimensions, finish, availability, price and features with proper sources whenever
  possible.
- [OBR] (B3) Never invent brands, references, certifications or prices. If research is impossible or inconclusive, say so
  and give a descriptive spec for later research.
- (DC) Prices of what we buy come from the Hub materials (`hub_*` tables, read only; never names or prices of the Hub in
  the public repo).
