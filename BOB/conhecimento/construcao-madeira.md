# Timber-frame construction — technical reference

General practice and regulations (not DiscoverCasa decisions — those are in `sistema-construtivo.md`). Condensed
2026-10-28 (history in git).

## 1. Method (light timber frame / platform frame)
- Walls = studs ("montantes") between a bottom plate ("travessa inferior" / "soleira") and a top plate ("travessa
  superior" / "frechal"), noggings ("travessas intermédias" / "tarugos"), lintels ("vergas") over openings. Sheathing
  (OSB / plywood) nailed to the frame turns the wall into a shear wall.
- Platform framing: each floor is a platform; the next storey's walls stand on it.
- Load paths — vertical: roof → rafters → top plate → studs → bottom / sole plate → foundation. Horizontal (wind /
  earthquake): roof / floor diaphragms → sheathed walls → base; sliding resisted by anchors / angle brackets,
  overturning by hold-downs at wall ends and next to openings.
- Typical external wall (outside → in): cladding → ventilated cavity on battens → breather membrane → OSB → studs +
  mineral wool → vapour control layer → service cavity → plasterboard.
- **Notched beam ends at a support** (EN 1995-1-1 §6.5.2): a notch on the **underside** at the bearing concentrates
  stress at its corner and splits the beam along the grain — design shear reduced by k_v; a notch on the **top edge**
  over the support costs far less. Keep end notches shallow (remaining depth ≥ ≈ 0,5–0,6 h, a rounded corner) or have
  them checked. (DiscoverCasa notches its ceiling beams, at most, to fit — `sistema-construtivo.md` §2 (4b); form OPEN.)

## 2. Timber ↔ concrete interface (UK industry rules, good practice)
Sources: STA Advice Note 4 part 2 "Sole plate tolerances"; NHBC Standards 2025 ch. 6.2.
- Lowest timber ≥ **150 mm above finished external ground** (site tolerance −25 mm, never < 125 mm over > 50 % of the
  perimeter). Only with a drained detail and no standing water: ≥ 75 mm.
- **DPC under every ground-floor sole plate**, internal walls included, lapped with the slab DPM and the vapour control
  layer. Sole plates preservative-treated.
- Sole plate level ±5 mm over 3 m; line / length tolerance ±(√(4·d)+1) mm, min 5 mm (d in m); diagonals of domestic
  footprints ±5 mm up to 10 m, ±10 mm beyond.
- Sole plate overhang or set-back on the foundation edge ≤ 12 mm (≤ 20 mm for 140 mm studs); ledges forming water traps
  protected by membrane.
- Packing: ≤ 20 mm without both engineers' agreement; permanent packing = non-shrink grout full length or solid packers
  under every stud / post; no hollow plastic shims; grout not for gaps < 5 mm (< 10 mm hard to grout). Levelling:
  packers + grout after first lift, twin sole plates, or mortar bed.
- Anchors must resist sliding AND uplift; slab reinforcement coordinated with anchor positions. Holding-down steel:
  stainless ≥ 1.4301 or hot-dip galvanised 940 g/m² per side; anchors inside the envelope ≥ Z275.
- Breather membrane Sd < 0,12 m (0,6 MNs/g), class W2 (W1 with open-jointed cladding), laps 100 mm horizontal / 150 mm
  vertical, fixings ≤ 600 mm h / 300 mm v, lapped to protect the sole plate.
- Vapour control layer on the warm side (e.g. 125 µm PE, 100 mm laps), installed when timber MC < 20 %.
- Cavities behind cladding: ≥ 50 mm masonry, ≥ 25 mm render on lath, ≥ 15 mm other claddings; drained and vented at
  the base; battens over studs; counter-battens for horizontal battens. Where horizontal battens butt vertical ones
  (corners, jambs) and at the ends of head / sill battens, leave 10–20 mm gaps (or notches) so no cavity bay is closed on
  all sides.
- Timber never below ground or retaining earth (concrete where there is soil contact).

## 3. Portuguese regulations
- Despacho Normativo 21/2019 (DR 17-09-2019, in force 16-12-2019): Eurocodes mandatory for building structures after a
  3-year transition (ended Sept 2022). Annex I for all buildings: NP EN 1990, NP EN 1991-1-1/-1-2/-1-3/-1-4/-1-5,
  NP EN 1997-1, NP EN 1998-1/-3/-5. EC2 / EC3 for concrete / steel.
- Timber design: **NP EN 1995-1-1:2022** (general) and **NP EN 1995-1-2:2022** (fire), with Portuguese National
  Annexes (LNEC keeps the list of published Eurocodes).
- Seismic: NP EN 1998-1 with PT annex (two action types, zones by municipality). The Lisbon / lower Tagus region is among
  the higher-hazard areas of mainland Portugal — shear walls, hold-downs and anchorage matter there.
- Licensing: RJUE; specialties include stability (estabilidade), water / sewage, electrical, ITED, thermal (energy
  performance), acoustics, fire safety (SCIE).
- Clear height (pé-direito livre), RGEU (DL 38 382/1951) art. 65.º: dwellings ≥ **2,40 m** (and ≥ 2,70 floor to floor),
  ≥ 2,20 m in vestíbulos, corredores, instalações sanitárias, despensas and arrecadações; shops ≥ 3,0 m (general
  knowledge — check the current text and the municipal rules).
- Accessibility: DL 163/2006 — doors ≥ 0,77 m clear, ≥ 0,87 m at building entrances (check which applies).
- Rainwater: DR 23/95 and EN 12056-3 for gutter and downpipe sizing.

## 4. Durability and weather details
- Subterranean termites (*Reticulitermes grassei*) occur across all of mainland Portugal (LNEC database) and in the
  Azores (also drywood *Cryptotermes brevis* there). Treated timber + physical separation from the ground are essential.
- Use classes (EN 335): 1 dry interior · 2 occasional wetting · 3 exterior, no ground contact · 4 ground / fresh-water
  contact. Pressure treatment (autoclave) for class 3–4; anything in contact with concrete at ground level treated.
- **Parapets, copings, low-slope roofs**: a timber parapet is the most exposed part of the frame — a waterproof membrane
  over its top, lapped down both faces, under the coping; coping sloped towards the roof, drips ≈ 30 mm clear of both
  faces (or legs kicked out into a drip ≈ 10 mm off the face); corners and joints in one folded / welded piece, or lapped
  ≥ ≈ 100 mm and sealed — where laps are not used (DiscoverCasa's chapéus never overlap) the butt joints sit over a joint
  strip with sealant, over the membrane; metal copings come in ≈ 3–6 m pieces and aluminium moves ≈ 23 × 10⁻⁶ /K (a dark
  piece ≈ 2 mm per metre between a winter night and summer sun) → a small gap over a joint plate with a gasket, not a
  rigid sealed butt; over a ventilated cladding the cavity left open at the top under the outer leg with insect mesh
  (≈ 50 cm²/m at bottom and top). Where a roof meets a wall or parapet: a flashing upstand (≈ 150 mm) behind the breather
  membrane, cladding ≈ 50 mm clear of the roof with sealed end grain; every flashing in shingle order.
- **Sandwich roof panels**: minimum slope per the maker — usually ≥ ≈ 5–7 % for 5-rib panels (≈ 3 % for some
  single-length panels without end laps, some makers 4° ≈ 7 %), ≥ ≈ 10 % for tile-effect panels; the slope must survive
  the long-term sag of the structure below.
- **Box gutters** (behind a parapet): a fall to the outlets (no flat channel), a lining continuous up both sides, an
  overflow through the parapet lower than the lowest rim that spills inwards (in a sloped gutter, the roof-side rim at the
  high end). Outlets at the low point, the invert flush with or below the gutter bottom, a pre-formed outlet (spigot /
  bocal with a flange) bonded to the lining, a leaf guard, sized for the roof area (EN 12056-3); ideally a small sump.
  **Bottom outlet**: drains best but the pipe drops into the roof void and must turn to the wall — joints there leak
  unseen (one continuous pipe or sealed joints, a sealed sleeve through the wall). **Side outlet** through the parapet:
  keeps the pipe out of the roof void but crosses the frame → a sealed sleeve falling slightly outwards, the membrane
  taped round it, studs set clear of it.
- **Downpipes concealed behind a ventilated cladding**: the pipe must fit the cavity without cutting the membrane or the
  sheathing (flat pipe or locally deeper battens); joints sealed or none in the cavity; a sealed collar through the wall;
  a leaf guard and a cleaning access; sized ≈ 1 cm² of pipe per m² of roof (rule of thumb; a 35 mm cavity rarely gives
  enough without a local recess); every hidden gutter an overflow spout; the foot into the buried drain or a gully, or a
  shoe onto a splash block / gravel strip falling away — never discharging at the foot of a timber wall. An outside pipe
  with a box head is easier to clean and replace.
- **Window and door trims (remates)** in a ventilated façade: the trim hides the layers but does not seal them — seal
  the frame-to-opening joint in three planes (rain-tight outside, insulating in the middle, airtight inside — the RAL /
  ift principle), turn the membrane into the reveal and tape it to the frame; shingle order: a head flashing with a drip
  over the head trim, lapped under the membrane above; jamb trims over the sill's ends; the sill with an upstand, end dams
  at the jambs, a fall outward and a drip ≈ 30 mm clear; openings to the floor on a threshold with a fall and a tray.
  Aluminium touching copper-treated timber corrodes (separating tape, compatible fixings); flanges must leave the
  cavity's vents open.
- **Pergolas fixed to a timber-frame house**: posts use class 3 (4 near the ground) → treated, on steel post bases
  keeping the end grain ≥ ≈ 150 mm above ground / deck, caps on beam and post ends; a pergola touching the ground and
  bolted to the house is a **termite bridge**; its ledger is a water trap → stand-off brackets or spacers (no horizontal
  timber tight to the boards), every fixing through the membrane sealed, a flashing above, bolts into solid framing
  (ring, studs, jambs), or posts of its own at the façade. Exterior glulam: lamellae treated before gluing, or a
  solvent-borne preservative — a water-borne autoclave treatment of the finished beam can check it; seal site cuts.
  **Covered (tiled) pergolas**: clay tiles (Lusa / Marselha) ≥ ≈ 30 % per the maker (lower only over a continuous
  waterproof sub-roof — subtelha or boards + membrane — with tile battens at the tile's gauge), tile-effect panels
  ≥ ≈ 10 %; clay ≈ 0,4–0,6 kN/m² plus wind uplift (open on three sides) → beams, ledger fixing and hold-down bases
  designed; an abutment flashing at the wall; an exposed gutter with a fall, the downpipe into the drain, never onto a deck
  at the wall foot; a covered terrace adds built area and shades the rooms behind — check the licence and daylight.
- **Hips and valleys (rincões, larós) on sandwich-panel roofs**: (1) a valley or hip at a 90° plan corner between faces of
  slope s falls at **s ÷ √2** (faces 15 % → ≈ 10,6 % ≈ 6,1°); the panel's own minimum is checked on its face, the valley
  gutter and hip caps along the line at s ÷ √2; (2) every panel at a hip or valley is cut on the bias — triangular
  offcuts (≈ +10–15 % panel), the PUR core exposed along the cut (seal it, closures under the cap); (3) the valley takes
  two faces' water — a wide valley gutter (caleira de laró) on its own support, the panel ends lifted clear, sealed; the
  usual leak point → a downpipe near each valley foot; (4) hip and valley lines need a member (hip / valley beam, or the
  purlins mitred on a post) and the purlins on a hip end run the other way; (5) with one slope everywhere the ridge
  height grows with the span (9 m at 26° → ≈ 2,2 m above the eaves) — check the cércea / PDM; (6) a hip roof has eaves and
  gutters on every side and no gables; a cross-gabled roof keeps gables and fewer bias cuts.

## 5. Timber grades
- Structural timber sold in the EU must be CE-marked and strength-graded (EN 14081-1); strength classes per EN 338 (C18,
  C24…); glulam per EN 14080 (GL24h, GL28h…).
- Portuguese maritime pine ("pinho bravo"): visual grading NP 4305 gives C18 (EE ≈ C30, low yield); C24 only via machine
  grading (MTG, approved by CEN/TC124 in 2015) — hence imported Nordic C24 / glulam.

## 6. Connectors often specified
Rothoblaas: WHT hold-down (timber / concrete tension) · TITAN angle brackets (shear, timber–concrete) · WKR angle bracket
(hold-down on floors) · ALU START (aluminium base profile, lifts walls off concrete, ETA-20/0835) · screws HBS (partial
thread), VGZ (fully threaded), TBS (washer head) · LBA anchor nails · KOP coach screws · BYTUM bitumen membranes under
sole plates.
**DiscoverCasa's beam-to-beam connector**: the "estribo de asa externa" — an external-flange (face-mount) joist hanger,
galvanised steel (his photo: Esteba "estribo alas exteriores tipo B"), fixed to the header along the open side where a
wing opens into the body ("Prendem-se as vigas laranja", 2026-10-28; `sistema-construtivo.md` §6). General practice:
every flange hole fixed with the maker's nails / screws, seat width = the beam, height ≥ ≈ 0,6 × the beam; the carrying
member deep enough for the nails' edge distances (a header ≥ the hanger height — a 200 header under 220 beams is not
good practice). **Purlin ends against a rincão / laró member are also fixed with estribos** ("estribos.", 2026-10-28).
General practice: there the purlin meets the member at 45° in plan and the member slopes → skewed (and sloped)
hangers, or square hangers on a bevelled purlin end with packing — a square face-mount hanger does not seat on a skewed
joint; which product the company uses: not asked.
