- STA Advice Note 4, Part 2 "Sole plate tolerances" (structuraltimber.co.uk). Sole plate at least 150 mm above ground (a drained detail allows 75 mm). Level within ±5 mm over 3 m. Line tolerance ±(√(4d)+1) mm, minimum 5 mm. Packing up to 20 mm; anything more needs both engineers to agree. Levelling methods: grout, packers under studs, twin sole plates, or a mortar bed.
- NHBC Standards 2025, ch. 6.2 "External timber framed walls". Maximum sole plate overhang or set-back on the foundation is 12 mm (20 mm for 140 mm studs). DPC under all ground-floor sole plates, including internal walls. Studs at 600 mm centres maximum. OSB/3 or OSB/4 sheathing. Breather membrane Sd < 0.12 m (class W1 for open-jointed cladding). Holding-down steel: stainless 1.4301, or hot-dip galvanised at 940 g/m² per side.
- Portugal: Despacho Normativo 21/2019 made the Eurocodes mandatory for building structures once the 3-year transition ended (Sept 2022). It covers EN 1990, EN 1991, EN 1997-1, EN 1998-1/-3/-5, EC2 and EC3. Timber design uses NP EN 1995-1-1:2022 and NP EN 1995-1-2:2022 (fire), which include national annexes; LNEC keeps the list.
- LNEC data: subterranean termites (Reticulitermes grassei) occur across all of mainland Portugal, so timber near the ground needs treatment and protection.
- SerQ: visual grading of Portuguese maritime pine (NP 4305) only reaches C18. Machine grading (MTG) can reach C24. Structural timber needs CE marking to EN 14081-1.
- Rothoblaas (rothoblaas.pt) connectors often appear in Portuguese timber designs: WHT hold-downs, TITAN angle brackets, ALU START base profile (ETA-20/0835).
